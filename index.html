Case studies

What each project is, who it is for, the decisions behind it, and where it honestly stands today.

AussieBattlers

Problem
Artillery games are fun with friends, but most are built for desktops and don’t suit quick phone sessions or local humour.
Audience
Mates playing on their phones, in short sessions, who like a joke.
Product decisions
Touch-first controls for aiming and firing, custom weapons and chaotic maps, and a fictional-brand edition for public use.
What exists today
A multiplayer beta that has been publicly playable on a free host. The newer AussieBattlers brand and its domain are still being wired up.
My contribution
Concept, product direction, control and interaction design, AI-assisted implementation, and iteration from playtest feedback.
Next step
Confirm the public link and title, then keep tuning controls and reconnection behaviour from feedback.

Joshi’s Nightmare

Problem
Make a slicing game that feels immediate and fair on a phone, with a character that reacts to how you play.
Audience
Phone players, with iPhone as the primary target.
Product decisions
Portrait layout, swipe-to-slice with fast-swipe collision, a dose meter that drives escalating difficulty, and a sound bank tuned for low latency.
What exists today
A playable prototype in HTML5 canvas and TypeScript with unit tests and browser smoke tests. Difficulty is driven by the dose level (currently the dose-driven difficulty phase). The character art is still a placeholder.
My contribution
Game concept, feel and difficulty design, requirements, AI-assisted build, and hands-on phone testing.
Next step
Finalise character art and animation, then widen playtesting.

Trackn

Problem
Taste in music is personal, but services make it hard to keep your own catalogue or find who really shares it.
Audience
People who care about music and want to connect over it.
Product decisions
The user’s own picks and words are the core; the product is designed to be independent of any one streaming service. Connections are meant to be honest about versions and shared artists and songs.
What exists today
A Next.js and TypeScript codebase with a database schema, tests and a demo mode, still under active development. There is no public release yet.
My contribution
Product concept and decisions, requirements, and AI-assisted build with human review of the spec.
Next step
Reach a private test with real catalogues before any public launch.

Jellyfish

Problem
Family information (appointments, tasks, messages) rarely becomes anyone’s clear job.
Audience
Families coordinating day to day, starting with my sister Emma’s.
Product decisions
One loop: Information → Action → Owner → Time → Reminder → Completion. A Today-first view puts what needs doing now at the front.
What exists today
A Next.js, React and TypeScript prototype with a database planned for the backend. Sign-in and live data are not yet complete, so it is not a working app.
My contribution
Product direction and requirements, AI-assisted build. Emma’s interviews and research shaped the problem and should be credited alongside this work.
Next step
Complete authentication and the Today view with real data, then trial it with the family.

About

I’ve worked across customer success, product, operations and implementations, including at foundU. That means I’ve sat close to real customer problems and to the teams who fix them.

Now I use that to build. I prototype quickly with AI coding tools such as Claude Code, but the product judgment is mine: what to build, what to cut, how it should feel, and whether it actually works when I test it on a phone.

Outside work I’m into music and start-ups, and I like jokes that land.
